Rotator Cuff Rehab

Your personalized recovery program — week by week

🌅 Daily Morning Routine Every Day

Do these 4 movements every morning before your weekly exercises. They warm up the joint, improve mobility, and prepare your shoulder for the day.

💡 Tips

  • Move slowly and with control — pain should stay below a 3/10.
  • Breathe steadily; never hold your breath during stretches.
  • Warm muscles first — a hot shower works great.
Morning Routine · Exercise 1
Pendulum Swings
Lean forward with one hand on a table. Let your injured arm hang and gently swing it in small circles using body momentum — not muscle. Relieves capsule tightness and promotes synovial fluid circulation.
Mobility 2–3 min

Morning Routine · Exercise 2
Wall Slides
Stand with your back against a wall, arms bent at 90°. Slowly slide both arms up overhead while keeping contact with the wall, then return. Trains scapular upward rotation and shoulder flexion.
Mobility 2 × 10 reps

Morning Routine · Exercise 3
Scapular Retractions
Stand or sit tall. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and hold 3–5 seconds, then release. Activates the rhomboids and lower trapezius — critical for shoulder blade stability.
Activation 3 × 12 reps

Morning Routine · Exercise 4
Doorway Chest Stretch
Place forearms on a door frame at 90°. Step forward gently until you feel a stretch across your chest and front of shoulders. Holds 20–30 seconds. Counters the tight pec minor that pulls the shoulder forward.

📅 Week 1 Foundation

Focus on low-load activation and neuromuscular control. These exercises re-establish the firing patterns of your rotator cuff muscles without stressing healing tissue. Do 3–4 sessions this week.

💡 Week 1 Guidelines

  • Use light resistance — fatigue is okay, pain is not.
  • Keep movements slow and controlled (2 sec up / 2 sec down).
  • Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.
  • Ice for 10–15 min after if sore.
Week 1 · Exercise A
Band External Rotation
Anchor a light band at elbow height. Elbow tucked at your side, bent 90°. Rotate your forearm away from your body against band resistance, then return slowly. Targets the infraspinatus and teres minor.
Strengthening 3 × 15 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · Exercise B
Isometric Shoulder Abduction
Stand beside a wall with your injured arm against it. Press your arm outward into the wall with moderate force, hold 5–10 seconds, then relax. No joint motion = safe supraspinatus activation.
Isometric 3 × 10 holds
Watch Short
Week 1 · Exercise C
Scaption Raises
Hold a very light weight or band, thumbs up, arms 30° in front of your body (scapular plane). Raise to shoulder height and lower slowly. This plane minimises impingement while maximising supraspinatus work.
Strengthening 3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · Exercise D
Rows (Resistance Band)
Anchor a band at chest height. Standing upright, pull both handles toward your body, driving elbows back and squeezing shoulder blades together. Strengthens mid-trapezius and rhomboids for scapular control.
Strengthening 3 × 15 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · Exercise E
Serratus Wall Pushups
Hands flat on a wall, arms straight. Lean in slightly, then push the wall away by protracting (spreading) your shoulder blades — your upper back rounds slightly. This isolates serratus anterior for scapular stability.
Strengthening 3 × 12 reps

📅 Week 2 Load Progression

Now that your shoulder is firing properly, we introduce more dynamic load. Sidelying ER adds gravity resistance, farmer carries build shoulder girdle stability, and incline pushups introduce controlled pressing.

💡 Week 2 Guidelines

  • Increase resistance only if Week 1 exercises felt pain-free.
  • Sidelying ER: use a very light dumbbell (1–3 lb) — this is harder than it looks.
  • Farmer carries: keep shoulders packed down and back throughout.
  • Incline pushups: adjust the angle to control difficulty — steeper = easier.
Week 2 · Exercise F
Sidelying External Rotation
Lie on your unaffected side, injured arm on top, elbow bent 90° with a small towel roll under it. Rotate forearm up toward the ceiling, hold 1 sec, lower slowly. Gravity is the resistance — this directly loads the infraspinatus and teres minor.
Strengthening 3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Week 2 · Exercise G
Farmer Carry
Hold a light-to-moderate weight in each hand (or just the affected side). Walk slowly with shoulders packed down and back, core braced, eyes forward. Builds rotator cuff endurance and scapular stability under axial load — great functional carry-over.
Stability 3 × 20–30 m
Watch Short
Week 2 · Exercise H
Incline Pushups
Hands on a bench, counter, or elevated surface. Lower chest toward surface with elbows at ~45° from the body, then push up fully. The incline reduces load vs floor pushups. Finish each rep by protracting the shoulder blades (serratus push-plus).
Strengthening 3 × 10–15 reps

📅 Week 3 Sport Re-entry

This week bridges rehab to real movement. Exercises mimic sport-specific patterns — overhead pressing, rotational punches, and pickleball swings — all at reduced load to train the shoulder for what matters to you.

💡 Week 3 Guidelines

  • Only start Week 3 if Week 2 exercises are pain-free with full range of motion.
  • Overhead press: start with very light dumbbells (3–5 lb). Stop if you feel impingement.
  • Pickleball simulation: focus on smooth, controlled swings — speed comes later.
  • If any exercise causes pain above 3/10, drop back to Week 2 for another session.
Week 3 · Exercise I
Resistance Band Punches
Anchor a light band behind you. With your elbow at shoulder height, push forward in a controlled punching motion, then return slowly. Trains the serratus anterior and rotator cuff in the same forward-reaching plane used in racquet and court sports.
Functional 3 × 15 reps/side
Watch Short
Week 3 · Exercise J
Light Overhead Press
Press very light dumbbells (3–5 lb) from shoulder height to overhead with a neutral or slight inward grip. Lower slowly. This video covers shoulder-friendly technique and safe alternatives if you feel pinching. Build the overhead pattern before adding load.
Strengthening 3 × 10 reps

Week 3 · Exercise K
Pickleball Swing Simulation
Shadow-swing forehand and backhand motions at slow, controlled speed — no ball, no paddle impact. Focus on smooth shoulder rotation and wrist follow-through. Groove the movement pattern before returning to the court. Use a light resistance band for added shoulder activation.
Sport Prep 2 × 20 swings
