Rotator Cuff Rehab

Your personalised recovery program — week by week

Every Day

Daily Morning Routine

Do these 4 movements every morning before your weekly exercises. They warm up the joint, improve mobility, and prepare your shoulder for the day.

💡 Tips

  • Move slowly and with control — pain should stay below a 3/10.
  • Breathe steadily; never hold your breath during stretches.
  • Warm muscles first — a hot shower beforehand works great.
Morning · Exercise 1
Pendulum Swings
Lean forward with one hand on a table. Let your injured arm hang and gently swing it in small circles using body momentum — not muscle. Relieves capsule tightness and promotes synovial fluid circulation.
Mobility 2–3 min
Watch Short
Morning · Exercise 2
Wall Slides
Stand with your back against a wall, arms bent at 90°. Slowly slide both arms up overhead while keeping contact with the wall, then return. Trains scapular upward rotation and shoulder flexion.
Mobility 2 × 10 reps
Watch Short
Morning · Exercise 3
Scapular Retractions
Stand or sit tall. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and hold 3–5 seconds, then release. Activates the rhomboids and lower trapezius — critical for shoulder blade stability.
Activation 3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Morning · Exercise 4
Doorway Chest Stretch
Place forearms on a door frame at 90°. Step forward gently until you feel a stretch across your chest and front of shoulders. Hold 20–30 seconds. Counters the tight pec minor that pulls the shoulder forward.
Stretch 3 × 30 sec
Watch Short
Foundation

Week 1 — Foundation

Low-load activation and neuromuscular control. These exercises re-establish the firing patterns of your rotator cuff without stressing healing tissue. Aim for 3–4 sessions this week.

💡 Week 1 Guidelines

  • Light resistance — fatigue is okay, pain is not.
  • Slow and controlled (2 sec up / 2 sec down).
  • Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.
  • Ice 10–15 min after if sore.
Week 1 · A
Band External Rotation
Anchor a light band at elbow height. Elbow tucked at your side, bent 90°. Rotate forearm away from your body against resistance, then return slowly. Targets the infraspinatus and teres minor.
Strengthening3 × 15 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · B
Isometric Shoulder Abduction
Stand beside a wall with your injured arm against it. Press outward with moderate force, hold 5–10 seconds, then relax. No joint motion = safe supraspinatus activation.
Isometric3 × 10 holds
Watch Short
Week 1 · C
Scaption Raises
Hold a very light weight, thumbs up, arms 30° in front of your body (scapular plane). Raise to shoulder height and lower slowly. This plane minimises impingement while maximising supraspinatus work.
Strengthening3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · D
Rows (Resistance Band)
Anchor a band at chest height. Pull both handles toward your body, driving elbows back and squeezing shoulder blades together. Strengthens mid-trapezius and rhomboids for scapular control.
Strengthening3 × 15 reps
Watch Short
Week 1 · E
Serratus Wall Pushups
Hands flat on a wall, arms straight. Push the wall away by protracting (spreading) your shoulder blades — your upper back rounds slightly. Isolates serratus anterior for scapular stability.
Strengthening3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Load Progression

Week 2 — Load Progression

Now that your shoulder is firing properly, we introduce more dynamic load. Sidelying ER adds gravity resistance, farmer carries build girdle stability, and incline pushups introduce controlled pressing.

💡 Week 2 Guidelines

  • Increase resistance only if Week 1 felt pain-free.
  • Sidelying ER: use a very light dumbbell (1–3 lb) — harder than it looks.
  • Farmer carries: shoulders packed down and back throughout.
  • Incline pushups: steeper angle = easier. Adjust to your level.
Week 2 · F
Sidelying External Rotation
Lie on your unaffected side, injured arm on top, elbow bent 90° with a small towel roll under it. Rotate forearm up toward the ceiling, hold 1 sec, lower slowly. Gravity is the resistance — directly loads infraspinatus and teres minor.
Strengthening3 × 12 reps
Watch Short
Week 2 · G
Farmer Carry
Hold a light weight in each hand (or just the affected side). Walk with shoulders packed down and back, core braced. Builds rotator cuff endurance and scapular stability under axial load — great functional carry-over.
Stability3 × 20–30 m
Watch Short
Week 2 · H
Incline Pushups
Hands on a bench or counter. Lower chest with elbows at ~45° from the body, then push up fully. Incline reduces load vs floor pushups. Finish each rep by protracting shoulder blades (serratus push-plus).
Strengthening3 × 10–15 reps
Watch Short
Sport Re-entry

Week 3 — Sport Re-entry

This week bridges rehab to real movement. Exercises mimic sport-specific patterns — overhead pressing, rotational punches, and pickleball swings — all at reduced load to train the shoulder for what matters.

💡 Week 3 Guidelines

  • Only start if Week 2 is fully pain-free with full range of motion.
  • Overhead press: 3–5 lb max. Stop if you feel pinching.
  • Pickleball simulation: smooth, controlled swings — speed comes later.
  • Pain above 3/10? Drop back to Week 2 for another session.
Week 3 · I
Resistance Band Punches
Anchor a light band behind you. With your elbow at shoulder height, push forward in a controlled punching motion, then return slowly. Trains serratus anterior and rotator cuff in the same forward-reaching plane used in court sports.
Functional3 × 15 reps/side
Watch Short
Week 3 · J
Light Overhead Press
Press very light dumbbells (3–5 lb) from shoulder height to overhead with a neutral or slight inward grip. Lower slowly. Covers shoulder-friendly technique and safe alternatives if you feel pinching.
Strengthening3 × 10 reps
Watch Short
Week 3 · K
Pickleball Swing Simulation
Shadow-swing forehand and backhand motions at slow, controlled speed — no ball, no impact. Focus on smooth shoulder rotation and wrist follow-through. Groove the pattern before returning to the court.
Sport Prep2 × 20 swings
Watch Short

Temporary

Gym Modifications

You don't have to stop the gym — just train around the shoulder. Here's what to skip and what to swap in while you recover.

🚫

Avoid Temporarily

  • ✕ Heavy shoulder press
  • ✕ Upright rows
  • ✕ Heavy bench press
  • ✕ Lateral raises above shoulder height
  • ✕ Pullups
💪

Safer Options

  • ✓ Chest-supported rows
  • ✓ Cable rows
  • ✓ Leg work
  • ✓ Walking incline treadmill
  • ✓ Core work

💡 Why These Swaps Work

  • Chest-supported & cable rows — shoulder stays in a safe, supported position while still training the back.
  • Leg & core work — train hard without any shoulder load. Don't let the injury become an excuse to lose lower-body fitness.
  • Incline treadmill — great cardio with near-zero shoulder stress. Arms stay relaxed at your sides.
  • Upright rows — internally rotate and impinge the shoulder at the exact angle that aggravates rotator cuff injuries.
  • Pullups — high tensile load at full overhead stretch. Re-introduce only when pain-free overhead range is fully restored.
